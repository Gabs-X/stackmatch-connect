# StackMatch

A platform for companies to find developers and freelancers, and for developers to show their work. It is live at [stackmatch.dev.br](https://stackmatch.dev.br), in Portuguese and English.

**Postgres backend.** All data comes from the database: accounts, profiles, company details, saved talent, interests and meeting proposals. There is no fake data in the code, apart from the sample card on the home page, which illustrates the product with a fictional person; a new database starts from the seed (`npm run db:seed`). E-mails go out through Resend; the support assistant answers with Anthropic's Claude when the API key is set.

## Problem and proposal

Comparing professionals means gathering information about technologies, experience, projects, availability and hiring terms. StackMatch puts that data into profiles and allows a first screening through cards, followed by a look at the full profile and a shortlist.

The sequential discovery flow is inspired by swipe interfaces, applied to a professional/B2B context. The goal is to make the initial screening easier; StackMatch doesn't automatically rate how good a professional is.

## Initial audience

- Small companies, recruiters and technical leads looking for professionals for projects or hiring.
- Developers and freelancers who need to present their skills and a mini portfolio.

## Main features

- **Discover:** filters by technologies, seniority, work model, contract type, availability and city (pick the state, then a city or the whole state; several places can be combined). Chips remove selections. Profiles already in saved talent stay out of the list (those saved during the current round stay until the next one, so undo works). The server applies the filters and sends profiles 20 at a time; the next page is requested near the end of the ones already loaded.
- **Distinct actions:** pass moves on; save adds to the shortlist; interested records interest separately, also saves the profile and notifies the professional by e-mail (at most once every 30 days per company, even when marking, undoing and marking again). Pass and save notify nobody.
- **Jobs** (`/jobs`, in the header and in the company account menu): the company creates jobs or project scopes with a title, description, required and nice-to-have skills, seniority, work model, contract type, availability, city (optional, for hybrid or on-site jobs) and pay range (optional, per hour or per month). Up to 50 jobs per company; each can be edited, closed, reopened or deleted. "Search Discover" opens Discover (`/discover?job=<id>`) with the job's filters, which can still be changed, leaving out whoever is already in the job. Saving, marking interest or proposing a call from there, or from a profile opened from the job (`/developer/<id>?job=<id>`), links the developer to the job. The job page shows who is in it in four stages (saved, interest shown, contacted and call scheduled), which advance on their own and never move back; the company can also change the stage or take someone out of the job. The actions still count outside the job: the profile goes into saved talent, and the limits and e-mail notices are the same. When an interest or proposal comes from a job, the e-mail carries its main points (title, seniority, model, contract, availability, pay, skills and the start of the description, translated into the e-mail's language when there is a translation), the proposal preview shows the same, and the developer sees the job in Interested companies. What they see is a copy of the job at the time of contact (`job` column in `interests` and `meeting_requests`), so editing or deleting the job later doesn't change what was sent. Job texts are translated along with the company details and count toward the same translation limit.
- **Job match** (`src/lib/job-match.ts`): inside a job, each profile shows how well it fits, as a percentage, with the detail of each criterion (in Discover, next to the card; in a profile opened from the job, at the top). The weights are: required skills 40 and nice-to-have 15 (by the share of the skills the profile has), seniority 15, work model 10, contract type 10, availability 5 and city 5, only when the job isn't remote-only (full for the same city, half for the same state). Only the criteria the job defines count; an empty group ("any") is left out, and the score is the share of those criteria that is met. Discover opened from the job shows the best matches first, with the same calculation done in SQL (`rankedMatching` in `src/server/profiles.ts`), and the job page sorts by match or by order of entry. The score compares requirements with what the profile states; it doesn't rate the person.
- **Card navigation:** buttons and dragging, undo, an explicit end of the list and a manual restart.
- **Accounts:** sign-in, sign-up (company or developer) and password recovery screens, with per-field validation, an account menu in the header and a return to the original page after signing in.
- **Notifications** (`/notifications`, the bell in the header with the unread count): what happened to the account, newest first, 20 at a time: a company's interest, a call proposed, moved, canceled, accepted or declined, an application received or withdrawn (company) and an application that ended without them or whose job closed or went away (developer). Each one says who acted, the job and the call's time, in the screen's language, and opens the page it's about, marking it as read; "Mark all as read" marks the rest. Each account only reads and marks its own (the server checks the session against the account the screen shows).
- **Notification preferences** (in `/account`): the account's language (`users.language`, set at sign-up from the screen's language and changed by the header's picker or here), which notification e-mails go out in, and a switch per e-mail category (`users.email_off`): companies' interest; call proposals, new times and cancellations; answers to the times proposed; applications. All on by default. The center gets every notification either way. Codes, the notice of a changed e-mail and support e-mails aren't notifications and can't be turned off.
- **Account** (`/account`, in the account menu): change the e-mail (with a code sent to the new address; the old one gets a notice), change the password (other sessions are signed out) and delete the account, which also deletes the profile, projects, company details, saved talent, interests, proposals and the images in storage. Each action asks for the current password, and a wrong password counts toward the sign-in attempt limit. With `SUPPORT_EMAIL` set, the e-mail change notice uses the support address for replies, and tickets arrive at that address.
- **Sign-in attempt limit:** 5 wrong passwords for the same e-mail within 15 minutes block sign-in for that e-mail, even with the right password, until the oldest failure leaves the window; 20 failures from the same IP within 15 minutes, on any account, block the IP. The screen says how many minutes are left. E-mails without an account also count, so the block doesn't reveal which ones are registered. Signing in successfully or resetting the password unblocks the e-mail. Failures are stored in the `login_failures` table (`src/server/login-throttle.ts`).
- **E-mail limit per network:** each IP can cause up to 20 code e-mails (confirmation, password recovery, e-mail change) per hour, on any account, on top of the 1-minute wait between codes for each account. Beyond that, sign-up and code requests wait, and the screen says how long. This way nobody creates accounts or requests codes in bulk to burn the Resend quota and the domain's reputation. Password recovery checks the limit before looking up the e-mail, so it still doesn't reveal which ones are registered. Sends are stored in the `email_sends` table (`src/server/email-throttle.ts`).
- **E-mail confirmation:** after sign-up, the account gets a 6-digit code by e-mail and can only use the app after entering it on the confirmation screen (`/verify-email`). Until then, account pages lead to that screen and the server refuses its actions. The code is valid for 15 minutes, allows up to 5 attempts and can be resent every minute. Signing in with an unconfirmed account sends a new code.
- **Password recovery:** the person enters the e-mail, gets a code and sets the new password on the same screen; the account's open sessions are signed out. The screen responds the same whether or not the account exists, and a code for an e-mail without an account fails like a wrong code. The request takes at least 1.5 seconds either way, and a code e-mail that fails to go out only reaches the server log, so neither the timing nor an error tells which e-mails are registered.
- **Access by account type:** Discover and Saved talent are for companies only; My profile and Interested companies are for developers only. Visitors are taken to sign-in (returning to the page afterwards) and an account of the other type sees a notice. Each profile's own page stays open for shared links. Signed out, the header shows only Sign in and Create account; signed in, only the areas of the signed-in account.
- **Interested companies:** the developer's tab with the companies that marked interest in their profile through Discover or proposed a call, with the contact, suggested time, message and a button to reply by e-mail.
- **Company details page:** in Interested companies, the company name or the "View company" button opens `/company/<account id>`, with the description, location and website the company filled in under Company details, the contact (name, role, e-mail and phone), what it did (interest or proposal), its open jobs and the reply button. On each open job, the developer can offer themselves once ("I'm interested"), with an optional message of up to 1000 characters: they join the job as "saved" (or stay at the stage the company had already put them in), the company gets a notification and an e-mail with the job and the message (`Reply-To` set to the developer, in the company's account language), the job page shows "Offered themselves on <date>" with the message, and the jobs list counts how many offered themselves. A job outside the job board is only seen by those the company has already contacted.
- **Job board** (`/job-board`, "Jobs" in the header and in the developer account menu): the open jobs companies published, from every company, with the company name, city, pay, stack and details. The server searches (title as written or translated, company or skill, ignoring case), filters by work model (a job open to any model fits every choice), orders and pages them (`listJobBoard` in `src/server/job-board.ts`): 20 at a time with "Load more", and the count covers every job found. With a published profile, jobs show and, by default, sort by match with the developer's own profile, computed in SQL with the same weights as on screen (ties newest first); without one, they're newest first. Pages follow a cursor on that order, so jobs published or closed meanwhile don't make a page repeat or skip others. Only open jobs in the board are returned. The developer offers themselves from there the same way (a published profile is required). The company chooses on each job with "Publish in the job board", checked by default on new jobs; jobs created before the board were also published, and unchecking the option takes the job off it (`listed` column in `jobs`, migrations `0027_job_board` and `0028_publish_existing_jobs`). Closing the job takes it off the board. To tell the companies whose older jobs were published, run `npm run jobs:notify-published` (it only lists who would get what and shows the first e-mail) and then `npm run jobs:notify-published -- --send` (it sends; running it again sends again). It reads `DATABASE_URL` from `.env.migrate.local` and needs `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM` and `APP_URL` (the site address, for the link) in the environment; it only counts open jobs created before the board. The developer only sees companies that contacted them, which the server also checks; other addresses show "Company not found".
- **Company details:** a company account page (`/company-settings`, in the account menu) with website, location, description and the contact for developers (the employee's name, role, e-mail and phone). Meeting proposals go out signed with that contact, interests marked in Discover show the company's current contact, and replies go to the contact e-mail. Company name, CNPJ and sign-in e-mail are shown read-only. The CNPJ (Brazilian company registration number) is asked for at company sign-up, with a mask and check-digit validation; the test company account uses the sample CNPJ 11.222.333/0001-81.
- **Test accounts:** the seed (`npm run db:seed`) creates `teste.dev@example.com` (developer, with Gabriel Botelho's profile published) and `teste.emp@example.com` (company), both with the password `teste1234`. Sign-in is always by e-mail, and the sign-in screen doesn't show these accounts. Since the password is in the code, run the seed only on databases where these accounts should exist.
- **Full profile:** bio, role, seniority, experience, technologies and projects.
- **Saved talent:** compact cards with data for reference, access to the profile and removal from the shortlist. Each company account has its own shortlist, stored on the server (`saved_talents` table).
- **Profile creation:** a multi-step form (including profile photo, location, years of experience and contract types, used by the Discover filters), skills with suggestions, projects with description, technologies, cover image, repository and demo (with editing and removal), links, a preview of the card companies see in Discover and saving to the server on completion. Each save carries the profile version the form started from (`version` column in `developer_profiles`, migration `0031_profile_version`); if the profile was saved since, in another tab or device, nothing is replaced and the form keeps what was typed, offering to save it over the newer version or to load the saved one.
- **Profile drafts** (`src/lib/profile-draft.ts`, `src/lib/use-profile-draft.ts`): while the form differs from the saved profile, the page says the changes aren't published and keeps them as a draft in this browser, a moment after each change: the texts in `localStorage` (`stackmatch-profile-draft:<account id>`, with the version the changes started from) and each new image once in IndexedDB (`stackmatch-drafts`), by its contents, so typing doesn't write the images again; images the draft no longer uses are deleted, and one that doesn't fit is left out and the draft says so. Leaving the page in the app asks first ("Leave without publishing?", the draft stays); closing or reloading the tab gets the browser's own question. Coming back, the form offers the draft (when it differs from the saved profile) to recover or discard; nothing is published until the developer finishes the profile, and nothing is written over the draft until they choose. A draft that started from an older version than the saved one says so, and finishing it goes through the version check above. Publishing or undoing the changes deletes the draft the tab wrote (or recovered); a draft another tab wrote is left to it. Drafts are per account and only offered to the account that wrote them; signing out asks when there are unpublished changes and deletes the account's drafts from the browser, while a session that ended elsewhere (another tab, expiry) keeps them for the same account. Drafts expire after 30 days.
- **Skill suggestions:** while typing a skill, the field suggests those from the reference catalog (about 220, from development to infrastructure, security, design and management) and those already used in profiles and projects, most used first and then by name, with arrow-key navigation. If none fits, what was typed becomes a new skill and, once the profile is saved, is suggested to others. The same skill with another spelling ("react") uses the existing one ("React"), in the form and on the server.
- **Location:** the developer picks the state and then the city, from a list loaded from the [IBGE locations API](https://servicodados.ibge.gov.br/api/docs/localidades) (cached for a day). The profile stores `City, UF` (city and state code), and the server refuses any other format. An older free-text location keeps showing until it's replaced.
- **Required profile fields:** name, role, location, seniority, work model, availability, bio and at least one skill, marked with `*`. With any of them blank, the form doesn't move to the next step or finish: it shows the message on each field and moves focus to the first one. The rule lives in `missingProfileFields` (`src/lib/local-profile.ts`).
- **Profile without projects:** the Projects step is optional, with a "Continue without projects" option. On the profile page, a developer without projects shows a notice instead of the empty section.
- **Meeting proposal:** a company account suggests a time (date and time, in GMT−3) and writes an optional message. The professional gets the proposal by e-mail, with the same text as the preview the dialog shows, and replies straight to the company (`Reply-To` set to its contact): StackMatch doesn't manage the meeting or block time slots. The proposal and its e-mail are saved together; an e-mail that fails is sent again later (see Notifications). The dialog warns when there was already a proposal to the same professional. Visitors are taken to sign-in; developer accounts don't send proposals. The developer accepts or declines the time once, in Interested companies, until the proposed time; after that a proposal without an answer expires, the buttons give way to "The time passed without an answer" and the server refuses the answer. The company's contact gets the answer by e-mail (in the company's account language, with `Reply-To` set to the developer), and the dialog for a new proposal shows whether the previous one was accepted, declined, expired or is still waiting. Accepting a proposal sent from a job moves the developer to "Call scheduled" in it (`answerMeetingRequest` in `src/server/meetings.ts`).
- **Calendar invite** (`src/lib/calendar.ts`): when the developer accepts, the e-mail to the company carries a `conversa-stackmatch.ics` attachment (a 30-minute call, with the developer's name and e-mail and the job), and, until the call starts, both sides have the "Add to calendar" button (in Interested companies and in Sent proposals), which opens Google Calendar already filled in or downloads the same `.ics` for other calendars. The company's button builds the same event as the attachment, with the developer's name and e-mail: the Sent proposals list carries the developer's account e-mail only for accepted calls, and only to the company that sent the proposal (`listSentBy` in `src/server/meetings.ts`); the developer's button carries the company's contact (`meetingContact` in `src/lib/calendar.ts`). The event uses the proposal id as its `UID`, so importing it again updates it instead of duplicating it. In the `.ics` texts, any line break (a lone `\r` included) becomes an escaped `\n`, and the control characters RFC 5545 doesn't allow are removed (tabs stay). The call link is still arranged by e-mail.
- **Sent proposals** (`/proposals`, "Proposals" in the header and in the company menu): every proposal the company account sent, newest first, with the developer (linking to the profile), the job, the time, the message, when it was sent and answered, and the status: waiting, accepted, declined, canceled or expired. Filters by status (waiting, accepted, declined, canceled and expired) with the count of each.
- **Reschedule and cancel** (`rescheduleMeeting` and `cancelMeeting` in `src/server/meetings.ts`, controls in `src/components/meeting-controls.tsx`): either side can suggest another time ("Suggest another time" instead of accepting or declining, or "Reschedule" afterwards) for a call that is waiting, accepted, declined or expired, except an accepted one whose time has come, up to 3 times per call. A page left open until the call starts drops the buttons that no longer apply (add to calendar, reschedule, cancel, answer) at that moment. Rescheduling changes the proposal itself (`proposed_by`, `reschedules`, `update_message`), so the sending limits don't change and the new `.ics` updates the old event (`SEQUENCE`). Whoever didn't suggest the time accepts or declines it: the company answers in Sent proposals. Either side can also cancel a waiting or accepted call before it starts, with an optional message. Each change goes by e-mail to the other side, in its account language, with `Reply-To` set to whoever acted and a button to the screen where it is answered; when the company accepts a time the developer suggested, the developer gets the `.ics`. The job stage doesn't move back on its own.
- **Support assistant:** Ada, a floating chat with suggestions, streamed answers and a stop option, which answers questions about using StackMatch with Claude (details in [AI support assistant](#ai-support-assistant)).
- **Support** (`/support`, in the account menu, the footer and the chat): a ticket form with a subject (problem or bug, account and access, question, suggestion, other), title and message. Visitors give their name and e-mail; signed-in accounts get the answer at the account e-mail, can attach the conversation with Ada and see their own tickets with number, status and date. The ticket is stored in the `support_tickets` table and goes by e-mail to `SUPPORT_EMAIL`, with `Reply-To` set to whoever opened it, so the team can answer from the inbox; confirmed accounts get a receipt (visitors don't, so the form doesn't send e-mail to third-party addresses). An e-mail failure goes to the log and doesn't lose the ticket. Each IP and each account can open up to 5 tickets per hour (`src/server/support.ts`). The team closes and reopens tickets in the admin panel. When an account is deleted, its tickets lose the link to it.
- **Ticket panel** (`/admin/tickets`, in the account menu of admins): lists open, resolved or all tickets, with the counts, the author (name, e-mail and account type or visitor), the message and the attached conversation with Ada. Each ticket has a button to reply by e-mail (it opens the mail program with the subject `Re: [Ticket #N] ...`) and another to mark it resolved or reopen it; the ticket records when and by whom it was resolved, and the author sees the new status in Support. When resolved, the ticket sends an e-mail to whoever opened it, in the language it was written in, with `Reply-To` set to `SUPPORT_EMAIL` and, for accounts, a link to the ticket list. The notice goes out only once: resolving again or reopening doesn't send another, and if the e-mail fails the ticket stays resolved and the panel says so. Admins are the confirmed accounts whose e-mail is in `ADMIN_EMAILS` (comma-separated); there is no admin role in the database, and the server checks the list on every action (`src/server/admin.ts`). Other accounts see "Restricted area". It shows the 200 most recent tickets of each filter.
- **Terms of use and privacy** (`/terms`, in the footer and at sign-up, where it opens in a new tab): terms and privacy policy in PT-BR and EN, in `src/lib/legal.ts`. The text describes what the app actually does (data, providers, retention periods, LGPD rights); when one of those things changes, update it too. It doesn't replace a lawyer's review and doesn't identify the legal entity responsible.
- **Automatic translation** (`src/server/translations.ts`): when the profile or the company details are saved, the free texts (role, bio and project descriptions; company description and contact role) are translated between Portuguese and English by [DeepL](https://www.deepl.com/pro-api) (`DEEPL_API_KEY`; the Free plan gives 500,000 characters per month, and keys ending in `:fx` use the Free API), and only by it: Claude isn't used for translation. To use it again as a backup when DeepL fails or the month's quota runs out, set `TRANSLATION_BACKUP=claude` (with Ada's `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) and add Anthropic as a translator in the privacy policy (`src/lib/legal.ts`). Each text gets the other language, whatever language it was written in, and shows in the language picked in the header (`tv(text, translations)` in `src/lib/i18n.tsx`). Below a translated text, the profile and company pages say it was translated with AI and have a button to see the original. Translations are stored in the `text_translations` table, linked to the account and to the hash of the original text: only new or changed texts are translated, those of texts that left the profile are deleted, and deleting the account deletes them all. Saving waits for the translation (up to 30 seconds); if the translation fails or there is no `DEEPL_API_KEY`, the profile saves normally and the texts show as they were written. Each account can cause up to 20 translations every 24 hours (each save with new or changed text counts one, even if it fails), so nobody burns the DeepL quota by saving in a loop; beyond that, the profile saves without translating, and the new text is translated on a save after the window frees up. Requests are stored in the `translation_requests` table (`src/server/translation-throttle.ts`), cleared by the daily cleanup. Skills, names, links and project names aren't translated. To translate the profiles saved before this feature, run `npm run translations:backfill` (it reads `DATABASE_URL` and `DEEPL_API_KEY` from the environment or from `.env.migrate.local`); it doesn't count toward the per-account limit.
- **Footer:** on every page, with the Support and Terms links.
- **PT-BR and EN**, a responsive interface and accessibility adjustments, including labels, selection states and respect for the reduced-motion preference on the cards.

## Stack

- React 19 and TypeScript in strict mode.
- TanStack Start and TanStack Router for the app structure and file-based routes.
- Vite 8, with Nitro building the server for deployment (Vercel is detected automatically).
- Tailwind CSS 4 and components based on Radix UI.
- Framer Motion for animations and card dragging.
- Lucide for icons and Sonner for notifications.
- date-fns and React DayPicker for dates and the calendar.
- The Anthropic SDK (`@anthropic-ai/sdk`) for the support assistant.
- Drizzle ORM with Postgres (Neon in production, PGlite in development and tests) and TanStack Start server functions for the backend.
- Zod and React Hook Form for validating forms and the shape of local data.
- TanStack Query for server function queries, with loading and error states.
- ESLint and Prettier for checking and formatting.
- Vitest and Playwright for tests.

## Running it

Requirements: Node.js 22.12 or later and npm.

In the repository folder:

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the address Vite prints in the terminal. Without configuration, the server uses a local file-based Postgres (PGlite, in the `.pglite/` folder, created and migrated on its own); to start from scratch, delete that folder. A new database starts empty: run `npm run db:seed` to create the test accounts (with the dev server stopped, because PGlite only accepts one process at a time).

### Database

- `DATABASE_URL` sets the database: a `postgres://…` URL (Neon in production), `memory://` for a throwaway database, or the path of a PGlite folder. It is required in production.
- The schema lives in `src/server/db/schema.ts`. After changing it, generate the migration with `npm run db:generate` and commit the `drizzle/` folder. On production deploys, Vercel applies pending migrations before building the site (`npm run vercel-build`, which skips previews); if one fails, the deploy fails and the site stays on the previous version. `npm run db:migrate` applies migrations by hand, with the `DATABASE_URL` from the environment or from `.env.migrate.local` (see `.env.example`). Don't put `DATABASE_URL` in `.env` or `.env.local`: the dev server loads those files and would stop using PGlite.
- `npm run db:seed` adds the test accounts and Gabriel's profile to the `DATABASE_URL` database (read from `.env.migrate.local`, as in `db:migrate`) or to the local PGlite. Whatever already exists isn't changed.
- Step-by-step Neon setup on Vercel: [docs/deploy-neon.md](docs/deploy-neon.md).

### Checks

```sh
npm run lint
npx tsc --noEmit
npm test
npm run test:e2e
npm run build
```

GitHub Actions runs these checks on every push to `main` and on every pull request (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): one job with lint, types, unit tests and build, and another with the UI tests. No secrets are needed. When a UI test fails, its trace is kept as a run artifact for 7 days.

The UI tests (Playwright) start the dev server on their own, with an in-memory database, and run one at a time: before each test, the database goes back to the seed through the `/api/test-reset` route and gets the test data from `e2e/test-data.ts` (four developers and three companies that contacted Gabriel) through `/api/test-seed`; both only exist with `E2E=1`. If a server is already running on port 5299, it is reused and the reset fails; stop it first. The tests use the Google Chrome installed on the machine. Where it isn't installed, run `npx playwright install chrome` first. They cover sign-up with e-mail confirmation, sign-in and password recovery (reading the codes through the `/api/test-emails` route, which also only exists with `E2E=1`); access by account type; Discover (actions, undo, filters, a shortlist per company and paged loading); profile (links, GitHub avatar and fallback); profile creation with required fields, without projects and with a save from an outdated tab; profile drafts (leaving, reloading, recovering, discarding, a draft older than the saved profile, signing out and another account); each account's lists after switching accounts or with a failing server; Interested companies and company details; applications (following, withdrawing, the company's final stages, notes and history, and a list that fails to load); language and theme; notifications and e-mail preferences; the assistant; meeting proposals, including a call whose time comes with the page open; and the mobile layout.

To run the production build locally, use the server Nitro generates (`npm run preview` looks for the build in `dist/` and doesn't work with this setup):

```sh
npm run build
node .output/server/index.mjs
```

The server uses port 3000; for another one, set `PORT`.

Lint may show `react-refresh/only-export-components` warnings in modules that export components alongside functions or constants. They concern Fast Refresh in development.

| `src/lib/profile-api.ts`       | Profile server functions: one's own (read and save) and the published ones.                                                               |
| `src/lib/i18n.tsx`             | Dictionaries, the active language and value formatting.                                                                                   |
| `src/styles.css`               | Global styles and visual tokens.                                                                                                          |

Transient state, such as filters, the form step and the position in the list, stays in the components. Shared data uses Context and TanStack Query; only the language preference and the theme use `localStorage`. Accounts, profiles, company details, shortlist, interests and proposals live on the server; the shortlist and interests change on screen right away, changes go to the server one at a time, and the list goes back to what the server has if it refuses one, with an error message. Signing out or switching accounts drops the changes still waiting, and a list that fails to load shows the error with a retry button instead of an empty list.

## PT-BR / EN

The selector in the header switches between Brazilian Portuguese and English and stores the preference in the browser. Texts live in typed dictionaries in `src/lib/i18n.tsx`.

Profile options (seniority, work model, availability and contract type) are stored in Portuguese in the database and translated on screen, keeping the values the filters use. What each person writes shows as it was written, or translated as described in Automatic translation. Dates follow the selected language; prices stay in **BRL**, without currency conversion. Proposed times use Brasília time (GMT−3).

## Data and server rules

- **Accounts and sign-in:** sign-up and sign-in write to the database (`users` table), with the password hashed with scrypt, and open a session kept in an httpOnly cookie (the database stores only the token's hash, `sessions` table, valid for 30 days). There are two account types: **company** (with company name and CNPJ; lands on Discover) and **developer** (with a professional role; lands on the profile form, already filled in). The account records when it confirmed the e-mail (`email_verified_at`); codes live in `email_codes`, hashed only, one per account and purpose.
- **E-mails:** sent through [Resend](https://resend.com) when `RESEND_API_KEY` is set, from the `EMAIL_FROM` sender. Codes and account notices go out in the screen's language; notifications in the recipient's account language. Without the key, in development and tests, each e-mail goes to the server log (with the code); in production, sending fails.
- **Access control:** the `RequireAccount` guard (`src/components/require-account.tsx`) organizes the interface, and the server functions check the account type (`requireAccount` in `src/server/session.ts`). Shortlist and interest changes also send the account the screen belongs to, and the server refuses them when another account is signed in now (a tab left open from before switching accounts).
- **Profiles:** one per developer account, in `developer_profiles`, with skills and projects in their own tables. The `skills` table is the shared suggestion catalog, with one spelling per skill (unique index on `lower(name)`). The reference ones (`curated`) come from the `drizzle/0010_skill_catalog.sql` migration, organized by area; to add others, create a new migration in the same format. Typed skills no profile or project uses anymore stop being suggested. The photo is the one the developer uploaded in the form (cropped square and scaled down to 512 px in the browser); without it, the public GitHub avatar (`github.com/<user>.png`) is used, and without both, or if the image doesn't load, the initials show. Links open in a new tab.
- **Images:** profile photos and project covers live in file storage, and the database stores only the URL. The form sends each new image as a data URL when saving the profile; the server writes it to storage, keeps the unchanged ones and deletes those the profile no longer uses, only after the save went through. New images of a save that fails or is refused are deleted too. A URL is only accepted if it already is an image of that profile. Before writing, the server checks that each new image is a complete JPEG no larger than what the browser generates (photo up to 512×512, cover up to 960×600); if any isn't, nothing is written and the profile isn't saved. With `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` (the Blob store connected to the Vercel project), storage is [Vercel Blob](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob); without it, in development and tests, images go to the `.uploads/` folder (or `UPLOADS_DIR`) and are served by the `/uploads/...` route. In production, without the token, saving a new image fails. `npm run images:migrate` moves into storage the images earlier versions stored in the database as data URLs.
- **Company details:** one record per company account (`company_profiles`), read and saved only by that account. Until the company saves, the name, role and e-mail from sign-up apply. The developer only sees the details of companies that contacted them, through interest or a proposal.
- **Saved talent, interests and proposals:** stored in `saved_talents`, `interests` and `meeting_requests`, linked to the company and developer accounts; deleting an account deletes these records. Only published profiles can be saved, marked or receive a proposal. The interest shows to the developer with the company's current contact; undoing in Discover deletes the interest, but the notification and its e-mail will already have gone out.
- **Jobs** (`src/server/jobs.ts`): stored in `jobs`, with the stack in `job_skills` (linked to the `skills` catalog, which the daily cleanup doesn't delete while a job uses it) and each developer's stage in `job_candidates`. Only the owning company changes or deletes the job; deleting it deletes the stages, but not the saved talent, interests and proposals. Developers the company contacted see its open jobs and can offer themselves (`src/server/job-applications.ts`).
- **Applications and the hiring funnel** (migration `0032_job_applications`): three separate records.
  - _The developer's application_ (`job_applications`, one per job and developer): a copy of the job as it was, the message, the screen's language and a status (sent, in progress, hired, didn't advance, closed or withdrawn). It belongs to the developer and stays when the company takes them out of the job or deletes it (`job_id` becomes null). The migration copied the offers stored in `job_candidates` (`applied_at`, `application_message`, now unused and to be dropped later).
  - _The company's organization_ (`job_candidates`): the stage, now with three final ones (hired, didn't advance, closed) that only the company sets by hand and nothing automatic moves anyone out of, and a private note (`note`, up to 2,000 characters) no developer query reads.
  - _What the developer sees_: only the application's status, derived from the stage (saved or interest: sent; contacted or call: in progress; the final stages as they are), "job closed" while the job is closed or gone, and the events marked as visible. Taking someone who applied out of the job ends their application as "didn't advance".
  - _History_ (`job_candidate_events`): entering the job, stage changes (by the company or automatic), applying, withdrawing, removal, closing, reopening and deleting the job. The company sees a developer's last 100 events in the job; deleting the job erases its internal events and keeps the ones developers see.
  - _E-mails_: the developer hears when the application ends without them (didn't advance, process closed) and when the job closes or is deleted while it's waiting (not again if it was already closed); the company hears when one is withdrawn. Being hired, the other stages, notes and removals of someone who didn't apply send nothing.
  - _My applications_ (`/applications`, "Applications" in a developer's header and menu): the applications 20 at a time, newest first ("Load more"), with the job, the company, the date, the message, the status and the visible history. While an application waits on an open job, the developer can withdraw it after confirming; it's final (no new application for that job), and the developer stays in the company's job marked as withdrawn. Withdrawing and the company's changes to the job or to the developer in it take turns under the job's lock.
- **Contact limits per company** (`src/lib/outreach.ts`): up to 50 interest marks and 20 meeting proposals every 24 hours, and up to 3 proposals to the same developer every 7 days. When a limit is reached, the action is refused before any e-mail, and the screen says how long is left. Undoing an interest doesn't give the mark back: each one is stored in `interest_events`.
- **Simultaneous requests:** checks and the writes they allow happen in one transaction, with a lock per key where two requests could both pass the check (`lockKeys` in `src/server/db/locks.ts`). So requests sent at the same time can't go past the sign-in, e-mail, meeting proposal or job limits (the daily interest limit isn't locked yet, so marks sent at the same moment can pass it by a few); a code is used only once; a password or e-mail change doesn't leave sessions behind; a job stage only moves forward; offering oneself for a job sends one notice; and a profile save waits for another one or for the account's deletion instead of mixing with it. The translation of a profile version is only stored while that version is still the saved one.
- **Daily cleanup** (`src/server/cleanup.ts`): a Vercel Cron, scheduled in `vite.config.ts`, calls `/api/cron/cleanup` at 03:00 Brasília time to delete expired sessions, expired codes, sign-in failures, e-mail sends, old assistant questions and translation requests, interest marks older than 30 days, typed skills no one uses, notifications older than 180 days and the records of notification e-mails that finished (sent, skipped or given up) more than 30 days ago. The route only accepts the `CRON_SECRET` secret.
- **Notifications and their e-mails** (`src/server/outbox.ts`, migration `0033_notifications`): each event (a company's interest, at most once from the same company every 30 days; a call proposed, moved, canceled or answered; an application received, withdrawn or ended) records a notification (`notifications`) and queues its e-mail (`email_outbox`) in the same transaction as the change, worded in the recipient's account language (`src/server/notifications.ts`, `src/server/application-notices.ts`). A dedupe key per event (such as `meeting:<id>:accepted:<reschedules>`) makes repeating a request or running it twice notify once.
  - _Sending:_ the request sends its e-mails once the change is saved, plus up to 3 others that are due; `/api/cron/notifications` sends up to 200 due ones per run (daily at 03:30 Brasília by default, since Hobby plans allow one run a day; on Pro, set `NOTIFICATIONS_CRON` at build time to a closer schedule, such as `*/10 * * * *`). An e-mail is held while it's sent (`status = 'sending'`, `locked_until`), so two deliveries never send the same one; one whose delivery never finished is taken over after 5 minutes; and Resend gets its id as the `Idempotency-Key`, so a retry within 24 hours doesn't send it twice.
  - _Failures:_ a failed e-mail is tried again after 1, 5, 30 and 120 minutes, then marked `failed` after 5 tries, with the last error (no e-mail content) in `last_error` and the log. The change it's about stands either way: a proposal is no longer dropped when its e-mail fails.
  - _Preferences:_ checked when the e-mail goes out, so turning a category off also stops the ones waiting (`skipped`).
- The professional's summary shows their data; outside a job there is no match percentage (see Job match).

## AI support assistant

The button in the bottom-right corner opens the assistant chat, which answers questions about using StackMatch (accounts, profile, Discover, proposals, limits) with Anthropic's `claude-opus-5-5` model.

Only people with an account chat with Ada. For visitors, the chat doesn't show the message field: Ada asks them to sign in or create an account, with the **Create account** (`/register`) and **I have an account** (`/login`) buttons, which return to the open page after signing in (except on the home page). The route also refuses requests without a session (401), and the chat asks to sign in again if the session ends mid-conversation. The link to open a ticket stays available to everyone.

The assistant is called **Ada**, after Ada Lovelace. The persona (friendly, practical, using feminine forms in Portuguese) sits at the start of the prompt, in `assistantGuide`, and makes clear she is an AI: she introduces herself as one in her first answer, confirms it if asked and doesn't pretend to have human experiences. The chat screen also shows the "AI" badge and a notice that Ada is an AI and can make mistakes.

- **Backend:** the `POST /api/assistant` route (`src/routes/api.assistant.ts`) receives the conversation, checks its shape and size (up to 30 messages, 4,000 characters each and 30,000 in total, starting and ending with a message from the person) and streams the answer back as `text/plain`. If the Anthropic API fails before the first chunk, the route responds 502 and the chat offers to retry; closing the chat or stopping the answer cancels the call.
- **Knowledge:** the product guide and the rules live in the system prompt in `src/server/assistant.ts` (`assistantGuide`). When an app rule changes, update the guide too. The assistant only knows the open page, the screen language, the signed-in account type and the person's first name, which it uses to greet them; it doesn't read other account data or take actions.
- **Formatting and shortcuts:** answers accept a subset of Markdown (paragraphs, lists, **bold** and `code`), parsed in `src/lib/assistant-markdown.ts` and rendered without HTML. A link to an app screen, such as `[Open Discover](/discover)`, becomes a button that goes there (on mobile, the chat closes). Only the routes in `assistantPaths` become buttons; any other link shows as text.
- **Conversation per account:** signed in, the conversation is stored on the server (`assistant_conversations` table, up to the 100 most recent messages), saved after each answer. It comes back on reload, on signing in again or on another device, disappears from the screen on sign-out and is deleted by "New conversation" or when the account is deleted. Only the 20 most recent messages go to the AI. The guide is cached in the API, so only each conversation's own part is billed in full.
- **Cost and abuse:** each IP can ask up to 30 questions per hour (`src/server/assistant-throttle.ts`, `assistant_requests` table, cleared by the daily cleanup). Beyond that, the route responds 429 and the chat says how many minutes are left. The model's effort is set to `low`, enough for short answers.
- **Spending cap:** each answer adds the cost of the tokens it used (input, output and cache, at the Opus prices in `assistantPrices`) to the month's total, in Brasília time (`src/server/assistant-budget.ts`, `assistant_spend` table). The cap is `ASSISTANT_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD`, in dollars, defaulting to US$ 4. When the total reaches the cap, the route responds 503 until the next month, and the chat says Ada is paused and suggests opening a ticket. Support (`SUPPORT_EMAIL`) gets an e-mail when the month passes 80% and 100% of the cap. The cost is the app's estimate; for a guaranteed limit, also set the spending limit in the Anthropic Console.
- **Refusals:** the call uses the server-side fallback (`fallbacks: "default"`): if a safety classifier refuses, the API tries another model in the same call. If there is still no text, the chat shows a default answer.

To turn it on, set on Vercel (or in `.env.local`, in development):

```sh
ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=sk-ant-...
VITE_ASSISTANT_API_URL=/api/assistant
```

Optional: `ASSISTANT_MONTHLY_BUDGET_USD` changes the monthly spending cap (default: 4).

`VITE_ASSISTANT_API_URL` goes into the front-end build, so changing its value needs a new deploy. Without it, the chat uses the simulated answers and says the AI isn't connected; with it and without the key, the route responds 503 and the chat shows an error.

The URL can also point to another service with the same contract. The front end sends a `POST` with JSON:

```json
{
  "messages": [{ "role": "user", "content": "Como funciona o Discover?" }],
  "locale": "pt-BR",
  "context": { "pathname": "/discover" }
}
```

The response can be streamed text (`text/plain`, shown as it arrives) or JSON in the `{ "reply": "..." }` format; a 429 with `Retry-After` becomes the limit notice. The contract and the transports live in `src/lib/assistant.ts`; the interface, in `src/components/assistant-widget.tsx`.

## Visit analytics (Vercel)

The project includes [Vercel Analytics](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics), off by default. It only collects data when the site is hosted on Vercel; on other hosts, the collection script doesn't exist.

To turn it on in a Vercel deploy:

1. Enable Analytics in the project dashboard on Vercel.
2. Set the `VITE_VERCEL_ANALYTICS=true` environment variable and deploy again.

Without that variable, the component isn't included in the build.

## Technical decisions

- **Database data only:** the app has no built-in data (the sample card on the home page, with the photo in `src/assets/hero-developer.jpg`, only illustrates the product and isn't a profile); the seed is a separate script (`scripts/seed.ts`), run only where the test accounts should exist.
- **Context and TanStack Query:** they fit the project's size and scope without an extra state management layer.
- **Backend in the same project:** TanStack Start server functions, with Drizzle and Postgres, avoid a separate service and share types and validation (Zod) with the screens. PGlite keeps development and tests free of anything to install.
- **Session in an httpOnly cookie:** the token isn't reachable from the page's JavaScript, and the database stores only its hash.
- **Deterministic filters:** options in the same group use OR; different groups use AND. The availability filter matches the selected status. The city filter compares the profile location case-insensitively; a whole state compares the code after the comma. The query lives in `searchProfiles` (`src/server/profiles.ts`).
- **Separate actions:** shortlist and interest have distinct records, so organizing candidates isn't confused with intent to contact.
- **Shared components:** cards, controls and dialogs keep interaction patterns consistent across screens.
- **Explicit scope:** the missing integration (the AI assistant, without its URL) is presented as a simulation.

## Known limitations

- Accounts, developer profiles, company details, shortlist, interests and proposals live on the server, which checks the account type before reading or saving. Professionals aren't verified.
- The meeting proposal doesn't check the professional's calendar; the call link is arranged by e-mail, outside the app. Canceling a call doesn't remove an event already added to a calendar.
- Developer profiles live in the database (`developer_profiles`, `skills`, `developer_skills`, `projects` and `project_skills` tables), one per account and identified by its id. Each account edits only its own profile, and Discover lists those of every developer account, in the order they were published. For companies, each is a candidate like the others, with a page at `/developer/<account id>` in the same format as the rest; the "Your profile" badge shows only to the owner. Profiles that leave the contract type blank drop out of the list when that filter is on.
- Discover only shows profiles published in the database; with few profiles, some filter options return no results. There is no geographic search; ranking only happens inside a job (see Job match).
- Filters, the position in discovery and the undo history don't persist between sessions. The form keeps its content in state while editing and persists on completion.
- Accessibility and responsiveness got targeted adjustments; a broader validation with users, screen readers and different devices is still due.
- Automated tests cover the logic (Vitest, `npm test`) and the main browser flows (Playwright, `npm run test:e2e`).
